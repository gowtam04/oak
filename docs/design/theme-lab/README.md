## Signal (the hybrid)

Daylight structure + Index fact table + true Pokéball red (`#E3350D`) as a signal, not a costume. Red is Send, the live pip, the active mark, and the period in `Oak.`. Not a header slab. Not a clamshell.

## What changed

The first four replacements were too much world. This pass keeps one theatrical object (the live Pokédex) and three quiet product languages.

## The Dex (research)

In the original series, Professor Oak hands Ash a red clamshell. English dub nickname: Dexter.

Ritual, almost every time:

1. Flip the lid open.
2. A two-note ding.
3. A small LCD shows a two-frame sprite.
4. A robotic voice says the name, then the category ("Pikachu. The Mouse Pokémon."), then two sentences of fact.
5. Height and weight sit on a yellow data strip. Blue round buttons, cream interior, red lid.

Later regions change the hardware (Johto still red, Hoenn/Sinnoh get Dextette, Unova goes high-tech, Kalos is a holographic card, Alola is Rotom, Galar/Paldea are a phone app). The object people mean by "a Pokédex from the show" is the original red flip.

Oak the app is named after the man who invented that device. This theme makes the phone *be* that device. It is not Rotom. It is not a Game Boy. It is not merch plastic on every surface, only on the chassis.

## The five

1. **Box One** (kept) - PC storage, cool periwinkle, cursor.
2. **The Dex** - live original-series Pokédex. Theatrical on purpose, iOS especially.
3. **Daylight** - Box One's cool palette without the box story. A calm consumer app.
4. **Index** - competitive data without a stadium. Hairlines, tables, no LIVE pip.
5. **Guide** - curiosity as a field guide. Plates and a short entry. No garden, no moon.
